## Incident boundaries

An upstream `OAuth access token has been revoked` response is evidence of revoked authorization, not a detailed account-enforcement explanation. Local records, IP checks and fork comparisons cannot identify Anthropic's undisclosed reason or establish that spending, language, an IP change, or a missing patch caused it. Disabled accounts must stay disabled; do not retry them as deployment canaries.

“Unknown provider” in the console means missing slot/account attribution, not an unrecognized destination. Before attributing such rows to upstream traffic, inspect `request_attempts`, `attempt_count`, HTTP/terminal state and cost. Pre-admission quota/pool rejections have no upstream attempt and must remain visible as local failures.

The exact-IP guard still latches on a mismatch or failed probe. Diagnostics distinguish a deadline from TLS EOF; increasing the local deadline cannot repair an upstream connection that is closed early. Do not automatically rearm or change the approved IP after a transient recovery.

For stopped-only maintenance, explicitly record that the expected eligible-slot set is empty **before** deployment and preserve the stop/lock reasons. An empty gate verifies only control-plane maintenance, not model availability. Keep authentication, policy revisions, proxy bindings, per-account concurrency1, HOME and identity intact. Real JSON/SSE acceptance must be marked blocked when no authorized healthy account is available.

Anthropic's [authentication and credential-use documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance#authentication-and-credential-use) separately restricts routing requests for users through subscription credentials. Technical fixes, fixed exits and concurrency limits do not establish permission for that usage or guarantee freedom from account enforcement.
